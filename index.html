Getting There & Entry Requirements

Flights from South Africa

There are no direct flights from South Africa. The most popular airlines used to access Vietnam and Cambodia are:

  • Singapore Airlines (via Singapore)
  • Emirates (via Dubai)
  • Qatar Airways (via Doha)
  • Cathay Pacific (via Hong Kong)
Main international airports
CountryAirports
VietnamHanoi (HAN), Ho Chi Minh City (SGN), Danang (DAD), Hue (HUI), Cam Ranh / Nha Trang (CXR) and Phu Quoc (PQC)
CambodiaPhnom Penh Techo International (KTI), Siem Reap Angkor International (SAI) and Sihanoukville (KOS)
New Phnom Penh airport Phnom Penh's international flights moved to the new Techo International Airport (KTI) in September 2025, replacing the old Phnom Penh International (PNH) for international travel. Check that flights and transfers show the correct airport, and allow extra transfer time as it sits further outside the city.

Vietnam entry requirements

  • Visa: South African passport holders require a visa. The easiest option is the 90-day e-visa (single or multiple entry), applied for online at the official site evisa.gov.vn, approx. USD 25 single entry / USD 50 multiple entry.
  • Processing: officially 3 to 5 working days, but public holidays or corrections can add time, so guests should apply at least 2 weeks before departure.
  • Passport: valid for at least 6 months from arrival, with 2 blank pages.
  • Pre-arrival declaration (Ho Chi Minh City): since 15 April 2026, visitors arriving on a visa at Tan Son Nhat Airport (SGN) must complete an online pre-arrival declaration at prearrival.immigration.gov.vn. QR codes are available in the terminal for anyone who hasn't done it, but completing it before departure saves time.
Official sites only Remind clients to apply only on the official government websites. Many look-alike visa sites appear in search results and charge extra fees.

Cambodia entry requirements

  • Visa: South African passport holders require a visa. The 30-day tourist e-visa is applied for online at evisa.gov.kh (approx. USD 36, processing about 3 working days). A visa on arrival is also available at international airports and main land borders.
  • Cambodia e-Arrival Card: all travellers arriving by air must complete the free e-Arrival Card at arrival.gov.kh (or on the official app) within 7 days before arrival. It is not a visa, guests need both.
  • Passport: valid for at least 6 months, with at least one blank visa page.

Combining the two countries

Combining Vietnam and Cambodia is simple: short daily flights link Danang, Ho Chi Minh City and Hanoi with Siem Reap and Phnom Penh, and the Moc Bai / Bavet land border links Ho Chi Minh City with Phnom Penh.

Worth noting Visa requirements change from time to time, always double-check current requirements at time of booking.

When to Travel

Vietnam stretches over 1,600km from north to south, so weather varies by region. The north can be cool in winter while the south stays warm year-round, and the central coast has its own rainy season.

Vietnam seasons
SeasonMonthsWhat to expect
Peak SeasonJan to AprThe best time to visit Vietnam, cooler and drier weather countrywide.
Low SeasonMay to SepHot and humid with rain showers, but lush landscapes, fewer crowds and better prices.
High SeasonOct to DecPleasant temperatures and a popular time to travel (see the central Vietnam note below).
By region
RegionBest monthsKeep in mind
North (Hanoi, Halong, Sapa)Oct to AprDec to Feb can be chilly, pack a warm layer. Summer is hot with heavier rain.
Central (Hue, Hoi An, Danang)Feb to AugRainy season runs roughly Sep to Dec, with the highest flood and storm risk in Oct and Nov.
South (Ho Chi Minh City, Mekong, Phu Quoc)Dec to AprWarm all year. The wet months bring short, heavy afternoon downpours.
Central Vietnam in Oct and Nov Hoi An's Ancient Town and Hue can flood during these months. Clients can still travel, but build in flexibility, keep the central stay a little shorter, and consider a southern beach (Phu Quoc) rather than Hoi An for the beach finish.

Cambodia

  • Dry Season / High Season (Oct to late Apr): the best time to visit, especially for temple touring around Siem Reap.
  • Wet Season / Low Season (May to late Sep): rainfall is heaviest in August and September; the countryside is green and Tonle Sap lake is full.

Getting Around Hanoi & Ho Chi Minh City

  • Grab (ride-hailing app): the easiest and safest way to get around both cities. It works like Uber for cars and motorbike taxis, with fixed upfront pricing in the app (no haggling). Most short city rides cost USD 1 to 4.
  • Metered taxis: recommend reputable companies only, Mai Linh (green) and Vinasun (white), and advise guests to insist on the meter.
  • Cyclo rides: a must-do experience through Hanoi's Old Quarter, best booked as part of a tour with a pre-agreed price.
  • On foot: both city centres are very walkable. Hanoi's Old Quarter and Hoan Kiem Lake, and the District 1 sights in Ho Chi Minh City, are all close together.
  • Metro: Ho Chi Minh City's Metro Line 1 links Ben Thanh Market with the eastern suburbs, and Hanoi has two urban metro lines. Cheap, air-conditioned and a fun local experience.
  • Airport transfers: Hanoi's Noi Bai airport is approx. 45min from the Old Quarter; Ho Chi Minh City's Tan Son Nhat is approx. 30min from District 1 (longer in peak traffic). Pre-booked private transfers are recommended for arrival.
Crossing the road Tell guests to cross slowly and steadily, without stopping or running. The motorbikes flow around you.

Street Food & Markets

Hanoi

  • Old Quarter: the best street food destination in Vietnam, 36 ancient streets each historically dedicated to a trade, packed with food stalls, plastic-stool eateries and craft shops.
  • Ta Hien 'Beer Street': buzzing corner of the Old Quarter famous for bia hoi (fresh draught beer) from as little as USD 0.50 a glass, with street snacks all around.
  • Dong Xuan Market: Hanoi's largest covered market, with fabrics, souvenirs and a famous food alley behind it.
  • Hanoi Weekend Night Market: Friday to Sunday evenings, from Hang Dao Street to Dong Xuan, with souvenirs, clothing and street food; great for browsing after dinner.

Ho Chi Minh City

  • Ben Thanh Market (District 1): the city's iconic market for souvenirs, coffee, spices, textiles and a food section; the surrounding night market comes alive in the evening. Bargaining is expected.
  • Ho Thi Ky Food Street (District 10): the city's best dedicated street food alley, with dozens of vendors serving Vietnamese and Cambodian-influenced snacks.
  • Binh Tay Market (Cholon / Chinatown): a more local wholesale market experience in the historic Chinatown, combine with a Chinatown tour.
  • Bui Vien Walking Street: lively evening street with bars and street eats, best for younger clients.

Recommended street food to try

Pho

Vietnam's national dish, aromatic beef or chicken noodle soup eaten for breakfast by locals (USD 1.50 to 3).

Banh Mi

Crispy baguette stuffed with pâté, pork, pickled vegetables and coriander, for around USD 1 to 2.

Bun Cha (Hanoi)

Grilled pork with rice noodles and dipping sauce, famously enjoyed by President Obama in Hanoi.

Egg Coffee / Ca Phe Trung (Hanoi)

A rich Hanoi speciality, coffee topped with whipped egg yolk and condensed milk cream.

Goi Cuon

Fresh rice-paper spring rolls with prawn, pork and herbs, light and healthy.

Banh Xeo (South)

Sizzling crispy pancake stuffed with pork, prawns and beansprouts, wrapped in lettuce and herbs.

Com Tam (Ho Chi Minh City)

'Broken rice' with grilled pork chop, a Saigon classic.

Cao Lau (Hoi An)

Hoi An's signature noodle dish, only made properly with local well water.

Che

Colourful Vietnamese sweet dessert 'soups' with beans, jellies and coconut milk, a perfect market snack.

Worth noting Advise guests to choose busy stalls with high turnover, eat food cooked fresh to order, and carry small notes as vendors are cash-only.

Cambodia: Siem Reap & Phnom Penh

Siem Reap The highlight of Cambodia
  • The most popular and most visited destination in Cambodia, gateway to the temples of Angkor.
  • Highlights: the Angkor temples (UNESCO World Heritage) including Angkor Wat, Angkor Thom, Bayon and Ta Prohm; Tonle Sap floating villages (Kampong Phluk stilt village); monk blessing at Wat Damnak; handicraft-making and the arts.
  • Getting here: 1hr flight from Phnom Penh or Sihanoukville; daily international flights from Vietnam and Thailand.
  • Airport: Siem Reap Angkor International (SAI) is about 1hr by road from town, so pre-book the transfer.
  • Getting around: tuk-tuks are the classic way to get around town and between temples, and the Grab app also works in Siem Reap and Phnom Penh. Temple days are best done with a guide and driver.

PassApprox. price

Check whether the pass is already included in the DMC programme before quoting it as an extra. Shoulders and knees must be covered inside the temples.

Phnom Penh Capital city
  • Cambodia's riverside capital, at the meeting of the Mekong and Tonle Sap rivers.
  • Highlights: the Royal Palace and Silver Pagoda, the National Museum, the Central Market and the riverside promenade.
  • History: the Tuol Sleng Genocide Museum and the Choeung Ek memorial (the Killing Fields) tell the story of the Khmer Rouge years. These are moving visits, best with a guide, and not suitable for young children.
  • Getting here: 1hr flight from Siem Reap; or overland from Ho Chi Minh City via the Moc Bai / Bavet border.

Travel & Cultural Tips

Money

  • Vietnam: carry Dong in small notes for street food, markets and taxis. Cards are fine in hotels and larger restaurants, and ATMs are easy to find in the cities.
  • Cambodia: US Dollars are used everywhere, with small change often given in Riel. Bring clean, untorn USD notes, as damaged notes are often refused.
  • Bargaining: expected at markets, keep it friendly and smile.
  • Tipping: not traditional, but appreciated for guides, drivers and good service.

Practical

  • Plugs: South African plugs won't fit, pack a universal travel adaptor.
  • Health: advise clients to visit a travel clinic 4 to 6 weeks before departure for current vaccine and medical advice, and to drink bottled water.
  • Travel insurance: strongly recommended for every booking.

Temples & etiquette

  • Cover shoulders and knees when visiting temples and pagodas, including Angkor Wat. A light scarf or sarong is handy.
  • Remove shoes before entering pagodas and private homes.
  • Avoid touching anyone's head, and don't point feet at people or Buddha images.
  • In Cambodia, women should avoid touching monks or handing items to them directly.
  • Ask before photographing people, especially monks and hill-tribe communities.

Handy phrases

  • Vietnamese: Xin chào (hello), Cảm ơn (thank you)
  • Khmer: Sua s'dei (hello), Or kun (thank you)

Selling Tips for Consultants

Sell the combination Vietnam offers cities, culture, cruising, countryside and beach in one trip, and Siem Reap is only a short flight away, making Vietnam and Cambodia an easy two-country sell.
  • North to south flow: route itineraries Hanoi, Halong, Hue / Hoi An, Saigon (or in reverse) using short 1 to 2hr internal flights.
  • Always include an overnight Halong Bay cruise: it is the highlight of northern Vietnam and far better than a day trip.
  • Recommend Hoi An for the longest stay: it consistently rates as guests' favourite stop, Ancient Town charm plus beach.
  • End with a beach: upsell a Phu Quoc, Nha Trang or Hoi An beach extension after the touring. Phu Quoc is ideal for families.
  • Book Bed & Breakfast only: as with Bali, steer clear of All Inclusive. Street food and local restaurants are a highlight of the destination and incredible value for money.
  • Include a food experience: a street food tour in Hanoi or a cooking class in Hoi An adds huge value at low cost.
  • Best travel window: suggest Oct to Apr for the best all-round weather across Vietnam and Cambodia. For Oct and Nov, keep the central Vietnam stay flexible and favour a southern beach.
  • Remind clients about visas: both countries require visas for South African passport holders, plus the Cambodia e-Arrival Card (and the pre-arrival declaration if landing in Ho Chi Minh City). Apply at least 2 weeks before travel.

Did You Know?

  • Halong Bay is made up of around 1,600 limestone islands and islets.
  • Angkor Wat is the largest religious monument in the world, and it appears on Cambodia's national flag.
  • Vietnam is one of the world's biggest coffee producers, second only to Brazil.
  • Son Doong, in central Vietnam, is the largest known cave in the world.
  • Every full moon, Hoi An's Ancient Town dims its lights and glows with lanterns for the monthly Lantern Festival.
  • During the wet season, the Tonle Sap river reverses its flow and the lake swells to several times its dry-season size.
